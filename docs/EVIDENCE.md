# Reasoning, evidence and research record

Let the question determine the shape of the reasoning. Avoid predetermining the answer or forcing
a fixed sequence. Preserve observations; test meaningful alternatives against appropriate external
or formal evidence. Revisit earlier constraints when new evidence matters. Uncertainty may remain.
Stop when further inquiry cannot materially alter the conclusion within the assigned scope.
Explain conclusions, decisive evidence, limitations and alternatives, not private chain-of-thought.

Every substantive interpretation records:

- Claim and status: observation, inference, contradiction, or unresolved.
- Source identity, version/time, record/line pointer and available hash.
- Confidence and its justification, including limits of coverage.
- Alternatives and what distinguishes them.
- Bias/fallacy risks, mitigation actually performed and residual risks.
- Disposition, verification needed and reviewer independence.

High confidence in a tool's existence does not imply high confidence in its reliability. A hash
links bytes; it does not prove the semantic truth of a claim. Two derivative reports from one
event are not two independent observations. A self-audit is not independent evaluation.

Journal protocol: date, human intent in anonymized form, initial state, decision/options, changes,
observed checks, failures, sources, limitations and next verification. Record superseded approaches
without erasing them. Avoid confirmation, survivorship, selection and model-authority bias; label
correlation/causation limits and known omissions. Do not assert a checklist removed all bias.

Manuscript progression: protocol/architecture first; reproducible implementation and methods next;
results only after measurement, with overhead, failure cases and threats to validity. LinkedIn
posts may explain tested milestones or design ideas, clearly distinguishing the two. Both use
reviewed public journal material, never automatic publication of private session logs.
