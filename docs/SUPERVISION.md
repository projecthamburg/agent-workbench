# Visible supervision and informed consent

The operator should be able to distinguish a teammate's report, a supervisor's
decision, and a notification generated by a monitor. Explain these mechanisms
before enabling them. A useful automation is not automatically an understood one.

## Notification provenance

A file watcher can observe a teammate's acknowledgment, deliverable and completion
receipt. A separate relay can notify the supervisor session. Its wording is
monitor-generated; the underlying evidence comes from the teammate. Neither event
proves correctness, authorizes another task, expands a budget or overrides a pause.

Record the originating role and task, source artifact and hash, observation time,
relay transport, delivery outcome and review disposition. Keep secrets and raw
conversations in the configured private runtime, outside this public repository.
Distinguish watcher activity, notification delivery, agent acknowledgment and
supervisor acceptance. A watcher alone does not wake every model session.

Session queues may defer delivery until an active turn ends. Stopping a relay does
not necessarily remove messages already queued. Check current human-control state
before acting on any notification; never treat an old return-control notice as a
new grant after a later pause.

## Visible terminal participation

When the operator requests visible supervision, visit the registered teammate tab
at a safe GUI boundary. Inspect its bell or unread indicator, current output and
input draft. Identify the recipient by registered session and terminal identity;
color and position are secondary cues. Opening a tab may clear an indicator, but
resolving the underlying question or reviewing the result is the actual task.

For a direct instruction, first retain its exact text in the supervisor outbox
and put a pointer in the recipient inbox. Inspect the draft, enter the instruction
through that tab, verify the full input, and submit separately. Confirm receipt
through an acknowledgment or native event. Preserve human or unknown drafts.
Clear obsolete supervisor drafts once their ownership and supersession are known.
Do not silently substitute a background queue for requested visible entry.

Terminal application badges can also signal unread activity in unrelated projects.
They are inspection cues, not task identities or success evidence. File receipts
and native records complement screen observation; none substitutes for all others.

## Changes, pause and recovery

Explain a newly proposed coordination mechanism and its effects before activating
it. Seek approval when it exceeds existing authorization or changes permissions,
costs, data handling or computer ownership. Routine actions within an understood,
authorized task do not need repeated approval.

A human pause takes precedence over task progression. Stop new dispatches, let
owned cleanup reach a verified boundary, and acknowledge handback explicitly.
Check durable pause state before every new GUI phase, including preflight actions.
Do not rely solely on a model session receiving its queued notification promptly.
The operator's return of control must be explicit; elapsed time is not permission.
