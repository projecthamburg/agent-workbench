# The operator must understand the automation

In the private pilot, teammates wrote task receipts and reports through a file-based
mail channel. A supervisor-configured monitor converted completion observations
into notifications in the supervisor's terminal session. This successfully brought
finished work back for review, but the operator initially understood those messages
as supervisor self-instructions or direct teammate messages. Their provenance had
not been explained clearly enough.

After clarification, the operator endorsed the mechanism and requested visible tab
visits alongside it. They wanted to see unread terminal indicators addressed and
instructions entered in the intended teammate's tab. The resulting contract keeps
both channels: visible terminal participation plus exact mail copies and source
receipts. Background command delivery is not a substitute when visible entry is
the agreed method. A new mechanism needs disclosure before activation, with
approval when it exceeds the understood authorization.

The same pilot exposed a pause gap. A durable human request existed, but it was
not checked at the next GUI boundary. A location-tool preflight screenshot was
taken afterward; no new regional resource query was dispatched. The eventual
handback included browser/process checks and routing verification. The mistake
was recorded rather than relabeled as timely compliance. Model notifications
alone are insufficient to enforce a human pause. Stopping a notification producer
also left previously queued messages to be delivered later.

Sources: private task receipts, matching mail pointers, native delivery events and
screen witnesses are retained in the pilot's private runtime. The public
[supervision contract](../docs/SUPERVISION.md) and
[coordinator boundaries](../docs/COORDINATOR.md) describe the transferable design;
private evidence and identities are intentionally not exported. These are local
observations, not a public reproduction dataset or a measured efficiency study.

Confidence is high in the observed delivery, disclosure and handback sequence.
Generalization to other CLIs and hosts is unresolved. We avoided treating a
notification as correctness, a return-control message as perpetual authorization,
and a stopped producer as an emptied queue. Remaining risks include delayed
session delivery, missed boundary checks, selective screen observation and a
supervisor interpreting its own pilot. No token savings, causal explanation of
all delays, or complete safety enforcement is claimed.
