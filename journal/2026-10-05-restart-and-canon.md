# Restart is a coordination boundary

The private pilot lost expected permissions when the operator changed macOS Privacy settings and restarted Terminal. Resuming native conversation IDs preserved history, but did not preserve every permission mode: the executor resumed with a workspace sandbox and could not write shared mail; the curator resumed with interactive tool prompts. The supervisor restored the explicitly authorized tool settings and required removable write probes from each role. This distinguished native identity, tool policy, OS privacy and administrator authentication instead of treating them as one permission.

A delayed return-control event arrived after a newer pause. The supervisor compared durable receipt timestamps and preserved the newer pause. The workbench panel was updated from the matching request, rather than letting arrival order authorize work. A later explicit handback resumed only saved tasks.

The interruption review also exposed a supervisor relay error: selecting a teammate tab in one call and typing in a later call could target the supervisor tab after focus changed. Guarded selection, identity verification and entry in one operation, followed by a screen witness, corrected observed delivery. This remains cooperative automation, not an OS guarantee.

For the report-card canon, immutable observations and separate independent reviews are retained. Ratings are bound to card hashes; both role ratings produce the average, while evidence gates separately determine route eligibility. Negative records remain useful history. A nearby working recipe is method guidance, not proof of the requested city's query exit. Daily mail publication does not wake a model; the supervisor must relay and observe an actual acknowledgement.

Sources: private restart-check ACKs, matching panel receipts, guarded-tab screenshots, native session headers, report_card_canon implementation and its 24 passing unittest cases. The evidence is retained privately; no personal identifiers, credentials or private transcript excerpts are exported here.

Confidence: high for the observed permission/write-probe and receipt ordering results; moderate for robustness across other terminal layouts and CLI releases. Avoided fallacies include history-resumed therefore permissions-restored, file-published therefore agent-active, high-score therefore qualified, edge therefore origin, and late-message therefore latest-intent. Remaining biases include a single host, self-evaluation by the supervisor, selective screenshot sampling and no controlled efficiency comparison. No measured token savings or universal reliability is claimed.

A later live frontend inspection exposed another focus race: a browser launched after a Terminal tab was selected, between paste and Enter. Global keystrokes can be misdirected even when an earlier identity check passed. The supervisor recorded the interference, ceased GUI input until the executor handed it back, and required the executor to inspect retained requests and final composers. No known generation request or instruction text was observed; an initial startup capture gap remained explicit. Absence in captured traffic is not a proof of universal absence. Future relays require no active browser/input phase and a new frontmost/tab check immediately before paste and Enter. These safeguards reduce observed failure modes; they do not establish OS-level exclusion.

Independent review distinguished three approvals: an outcome label was supported, capture mechanics were verified, and an answer was judged adequate. Scores and label acceptance cannot replace an explicit answer-adequacy disposition. Freshness must come from sealed observation times rather than a newly written report. Historical corrections append revisions bound to prior hashes; they do not edit the earlier observation or inherit its reviews. The private archive currently has19 immutable cards and zero qualified routes under the new contract. That is a coverage statement, not evidence that resources never work.

Local browser fixtures also produced an informative false failure. JavaScript referred to the browser global `window.stop` rather than the fixture's Stop button. The failed batch was preserved, the fixture corrected, and six fresh-profile desktop/tablet/mobile checks in two browsers then passed. The fixture proves local browser mechanics and cleanup; it does not prove provider attachment acceptance or answer quality. Other installed browsers exposed actual-profile and startup problems that remain recorded.
